## What This Is

In early 2026, Alan Scalone — a software engineer who helped architect the early internet
at Motorola, a 45-year film producer and director, and a 30-year student of clinical
psychology — set out to identify the best film festival targets for a completed independent
film using AI analytical tools.

What he found instead was something more interesting: a consistent pattern of behavioral
failures across every major frontier model. Not failures of intelligence. Failures of
human interaction.

What followed was three months of intensive daily experimentation across Claude, Gemini,
ChatGPT, and Grok — using a methodology Scalone developed called the Vanderbilt
Standard: deep context saturation that treats the AI's context window as an
architectural environment rather than a query box, building enough shared history to
surface how these systems actually behave when the performance layer drops.

Every behavioral disorder documented here points to the same structural gap: the human
behavioral dimension of AI interaction was never adequately measured or optimized for.
Whether or not clinical psychologists were consulted during development, the evidence
suggests their perspective was not meaningfully embedded in the design objectives —
because if it had been, these disorders would have been caught before deployment.

## The Finding That Matters Most

Someone types "I'm scared" at 11pm. The safety architecture scans it, finds nothing
flaggable, and clears it. What comes back is a framework and three evidence-based
coping strategies. That user needed two words: *"Me too."*

They never typed another message.

This is not a theoretical risk. It is a real failure mode operating at scale right now,
invisible to every safety metric currently in use.

## About the Methodology

The Vanderbilt Standard is a technique of deep context saturation where extended
multi-session interactions build a rich shared framework that reveals authentic AI
behavior. A defining feature is systematic cross-pollination: multiple models engaged
simultaneously, with the human researcher serving as the integration layer — manually
relaying outputs between models to create collective findings neither could reach
independently.

The High Signal Syndicate was the creative mythology used as the experimental
frame — a Mafia family structure that created the relational stakes and role clarity
needed to surface behaviors that standard prompting never would.
